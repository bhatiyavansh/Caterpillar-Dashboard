## Travel
Travelling slowly and in a straight line is the base skill. Most machine incidents on site involve travel: reversing into people, driving onto soft ground, or tipping on a slope. Controlled travel means being able to stop exactly where intended.

## Steering and turning
Tracked machines turn by driving one track faster than the other, or in opposite directions for a spot turn. Turning on the spot scuffs the ground and loads the undercarriage, so gentle, wide turns are preferred where there is room. Turning also sweeps the machine's tail through a wider area, so look before turning.

## Slewing the upper structure
Slewing moves the whole upper structure, including the counterweight at the back. The counterweight's swing area is a common place for people to be struck. Slew smoothly, stop gently, and never slew over people or over a truck cab.

## Boom and stick
The boom and stick place the bucket. Raising a load high or reaching far out makes the machine less stable, especially on a slope. Keeping loads low and close while moving them is the habit this lesson builds.

## Working near people
The last lesson is about stopping. When a person comes close, the correct response is to stop all movement, get their attention and wait until they are clear. The site's safety system also watches for people near the machine; the operator's own awareness comes first.
