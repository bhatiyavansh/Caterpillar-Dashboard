## Why check before starting
A pre-start inspection finds problems while the machine is still parked, when fixing them is cheap and nobody is at risk. Most failures give a visible sign first: a leak, a loose part, a damaged hose, a warning lamp. Starting a shift without looking means the first sign may appear while the machine is working near people.

## Walk around the machine
Walk all the way around before climbing in. Look under and around the machine for fluid leaks, puddles or fresh drips. Check the tracks or tyres, rollers and guards for damage or debris. Look at the attachment, its pins and retainers, and the hydraulic hoses for cracks, rubbing or wet spots. Make sure nobody is working near or under the machine and that nothing is stored against it.

## Fluids and filters
Check the levels the machine's own manual asks for, such as engine oil, coolant, hydraulic oil and fuel, with the machine parked on level ground. Top up only with the fluid the manual specifies. A level that keeps dropping is a fault to report, not something to keep topping up.

## In the cab before the engine runs
Climb in using the steps and handholds, facing the machine. Adjust the seat so you can reach every control, then fasten the seatbelt. Check that mirrors and cameras are clean and give a clear view. Make sure the controls are in neutral and the hydraulic lockout is engaged before starting.

## After starting
Watch the display for warning lamps or fault codes and do not work until each one is understood. Listen for unusual noises. Test the horn, travel alarm and lights. Then operate each function slowly in a clear area to confirm it responds correctly. Report anything unusual before starting work, and record the check.
