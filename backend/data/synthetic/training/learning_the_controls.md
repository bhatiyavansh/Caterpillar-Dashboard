## One function at a time
New operators learn fastest by moving one function at a time: travel, then steering, then slew, then boom and stick. Combining functions smoothly comes later, once each single movement is predictable. Trying to do everything at once makes it hard to know which input caused which movement.

## Smooth, small inputs
Hydraulic controls respond in proportion to how far they are moved. Small, steady inputs give slow, controllable motion; sudden full inputs make the machine jerk, spill loads and unsettle its stability. Ease a control in, hold it, and ease it out. Smoothness matters more than speed while learning.

## Know which way is forward
On a tracked machine the upper structure can face a different direction from the tracks. Before travelling, check where the travel motors or drive sprockets are. If they are in front of you, the travel controls work in the opposite sense to what you expect. Always confirm the direction before moving.

## Look before every movement
Before each travel or slew movement, look in the direction the machine will move and check mirrors and cameras. The rear of an excavator's upper structure swings out beyond the tracks when slewing, so the area behind and beside the machine must be clear as well.

## Practising in a simulator
A simulator lets a trainee repeat movements without risk. In this training system each lesson step is checked by the simulated machine's sensors: the step passes only when the machine actually does what was asked, for long enough. The instructor explains and encourages, but the sensors decide whether a step passed.
