## What hydraulic temperature tells you
Hydraulic oil heats up under continuous heavy work, in hot weather, or when cooling is restricted. Hot oil thins, seals and hoses age faster, and performance drops. A rising temperature trend is a warning to act on before it becomes a fault.

## Common causes of overheating
Long periods of full-load work without pauses, a blocked or dirty oil cooler, a low oil level, a slipping or damaged cooling fan, and internal leakage in pumps or valves can all push the temperature up.

## What to do
Reduce continuous high-load work and let the machine idle so the oil can cool. At the next safe stop, check the hydraulic oil level and look at the cooler for blockage. If the warning returns during the same shift, raise a work order. On this site the over-temperature fault code and its thresholds are in the Demo site manual (fault code HYD-118 page), and the hydraulic overheat protocol lists the steps.

## Other hydraulic signs
Slow or weak movements, jerky operation, unusual whining, visible leaks and wet hoses are also signs of a hydraulic problem. Do not look for leaks with bare hands: fluid under pressure can penetrate skin. Report them for inspection.
