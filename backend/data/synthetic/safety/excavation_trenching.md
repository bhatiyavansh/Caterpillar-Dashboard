## Before digging
Find out what is underground before the first bucket goes in: utilities such as power, gas, water and telecom lines must be located and marked. The site's permit or plan should say where it is safe to dig. Treat any unexpected pipe, cable or tape as live until confirmed otherwise.

## Trench walls
Soil can collapse without warning, and a small collapse can bury a person. Trenches that people enter need protective systems such as sloping, benching, shoring or shields, as decided by a competent person. The exact depth and slope requirements are set by regulation; see 29 CFR 1926.651 and 1926.652 in the document library.

## Keep weight away from the edge
Machines, spoil piles and materials close to the edge load the trench wall and make collapse more likely. Keep spoil and equipment back from the edge, and use a warning system such as barricades or stop logs when a machine works near an edge the operator cannot see.

## Getting in and out
People in a trench need a safe way out close to where they work, such as a ladder or ramp. Nobody should be in a trench while a machine is digging next to them or while loads pass overhead.

## Daily checks
A competent person should inspect excavations before each shift and after rain or anything that could affect stability, and stop work if there are signs of collapse such as cracks, bulging or water seeping in.
