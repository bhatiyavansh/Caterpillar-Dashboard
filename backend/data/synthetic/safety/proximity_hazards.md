## The danger zone
Every working machine has an area around it where a person can be struck or crushed: the path of travel, the swing area of the upper structure and counterweight, and the reach of the attachment. Blind spots mean the operator may not see a person inside this area, especially close behind or beside the machine.

## What the operator does
Stop all travel and implement movement when a person enters the danger zone. Use the horn and make eye contact before doing anything else. Wait until the person is clearly outside the zone. When a person was behind the machine, use a spotter before reversing. The site proximity protocol lists the exact steps to follow.

## What the site system does
On this site each machine has a safety bubble. A person inside the red part of the bubble raises a proximity alert from fixed rules and from the camera detector. The bubble sizes are described in the Demo site manual (safety bubble page). The alert is deterministic; the assistant explains it but never raises or clears it.

## Why it keeps happening
Proximity incidents usually come from assumptions: the operator assumes the area is still clear, and the person on foot assumes the operator has seen them. Both checking every time is what prevents it.
